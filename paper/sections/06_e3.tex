\paragraph{E3: Reader swap.} From the repeat-0 Full campaigns we drew 64 history packets with a committed seed, 16 per method on distinct sites. A packet holds exactly the public evidence the method had before recommending: every crop's policy, planting day, totals and margin, and a daily summary of every channel it read; it names no method. Each packet was read by the GP Reader (the baseline's own recommendation step) and by each model as a one-shot Reader; all 192 model readings returned a valid policy. The \emph{Reader swap gain} is the Reader's recommendation score minus the source method's own final recommendation on the same packet (sites averaged first; BH over the joint secondary family of \res{secondary.family} tests).

No Reader's pooled swap gain differed significantly from zero (Figure~\ref{fig:e3}). The one-shot model Readers scored slightly below the methods that had gathered the evidence, \res{E3.deepseek.mean}~\eur{} [\res{E3.deepseek.lo}, \res{E3.deepseek.hi}] for DeepSeek ($p_\text{BH}$ = \res{E3.deepseek.p}), \res{E3.glm.mean} [\res{E3.glm.lo}, \res{E3.glm.hi}] for GLM (\res{E3.glm.p}) and \res{E3.mimo.mean} [\res{E3.mimo.lo}, \res{E3.mimo.hi}] for MiMo (\res{E3.mimo.p}), and the GP Reader slightly above, \res{E3.gp.mean} [\res{E3.gp.lo}, \res{E3.gp.hi}] ($p_\text{BH}$ = \res{E3.gp.p}). By source the picture is mixed and, with 16 packets per cell, descriptive only: a model reading its own model's packets scored \res{E3.deepseek.on.deepseek} (DeepSeek), \res{E3.glm.on.glm} (GLM) and \res{E3.mimo.on.mimo} (MiMo); the model Readers did worst on GLM packets (\res{E3.deepseek.on.glm}, \res{E3.mimo.on.glm}) and between \res{E3.glm.on.baseline} and \res{E3.mimo.on.baseline} on the baseline's. As a consistency check, the GP Reader, which is the baseline's own recommendation step, returned exactly the baseline's final policy on all 16 baseline packets. Evidence quality cannot be compared across sources directly, because each source's packets come from different sites. E3 therefore does not show that reading the final evidence, rather than gathering it, limited the models.

\paragraph{Headroom.} A numerical search for the best feasible policy on \res{ref.sites} random test sites found policies \res{ref.headroom}~\eur{} above the fixed reference on average; two repeated searches differed by \res{ref.err.lo} and \res{ref.err.hi}~\eur{}, so the best-known values are lower bounds with that error. On these sites the baseline's final policies were \res{ref.baseline.vsfixed} relative to the fixed reference, most of the estimated headroom, while the models' were \res{ref.glm.vsfixed} (GLM), \res{ref.mimo.vsfixed} (MiMo) and \res{ref.deepseek.vsfixed} (DeepSeek): the models used none of it.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.} The size of the E1 gap depends on two environment assumptions. With the side-wall heat exchange set to 4~W/m$^2$K instead of each site's drawn value (1--3) on \res{ueff.sites} random sites, the model-minus-baseline differences shrank to \res{ueff.4.deepseek} (DeepSeek), \res{ueff.4.glm} (GLM) and \res{ueff.4.mimo}~\eur{} (MiMo), against \res{ueff.main.deepseek}, \res{ueff.main.glm} and \res{ueff.main.mimo} on the same sites at the drawn value; the method ranking \res{ueff.4.rankchange} at 4 and was \res{ueff.0.rankchange} at 0~W/m$^2$K (\res{ueff.0.deepseek}, \res{ueff.0.glm}, \res{ueff.0.mimo}). The models' policies use less light and more heat than the baseline's, and a larger exchange with the warmer neighbouring spaces favours them. With a fruit dry-matter fraction of \setting{0.07} instead of \setting{0.06} the E1 differences shrank to \res{dm.0.07.deepseek} (DeepSeek), \res{dm.0.07.glm} (GLM) and \res{dm.0.07.mimo} (MiMo); at \setting{0.05} they grew to \res{dm.0.05.deepseek}, \res{dm.0.05.glm} and \res{dm.0.05.mimo}. The sign of E1 held in every setting except MiMo at the highest heat exchange; E1 is a statement about the specified facility and price scenario, not a general ranking. E2 was insensitive to the dry-matter fraction.
