\section{Reading the evidence (E3), headroom and sensitivity}
\label{sec:e3}
\paragraph{E3: Reader swap.} From the repeat-0 Full campaigns we drew 64 history packets with a committed seed, 16 per method on distinct sites. A packet holds exactly the public evidence the method had before recommending: every crop's policy, planting day, totals and margin, and a daily summary of every channel it read; it names no method. Each packet was read by the GP Reader (the baseline's own recommendation step) and by each model as a one-shot Reader; all 192 model readings returned a valid policy. The \emph{Reader swap gain} is the Reader's recommendation score minus the source method's own final recommendation on the same packet (sites averaged first; BH over the joint secondary family of \res{secondary.family} tests).

The one-shot model Readers did worse than the methods that had gathered the evidence: \res{E3.deepseek.mean}~\eur{} [\res{E3.deepseek.lo}, \res{E3.deepseek.hi}] for DeepSeek ($p_\text{BH}$ = \res{E3.deepseek.p}), \res{E3.glm.mean} [\res{E3.glm.lo}, \res{E3.glm.hi}] for GLM (\res{E3.glm.p}) and \res{E3.mimo.mean} [\res{E3.mimo.lo}, \res{E3.mimo.hi}] for MiMo (\res{E3.mimo.p}). This holds even for a model reading its own model's packets (DeepSeek \res{E3.deepseek.on.deepseek}, GLM \res{E3.glm.on.glm}, MiMo \res{E3.mimo.on.mimo}), so a single reading of the final evidence did not recover what the campaign conversation had produced. The GP Reader moved recommendations up by \res{E3.gp.mean} [\res{E3.gp.lo}, \res{E3.gp.hi}] ($p_\text{BH}$ = \res{E3.gp.p}, not significant), most on language-model packets (DeepSeek \res{E3.gp.on.deepseek}, GLM \res{E3.gp.on.glm}, MiMo \res{E3.gp.on.mimo}; baseline packets \res{E3.gp.on.baseline}). Evidence quality cannot be compared across sources directly, because each source's packets come from different sites.

\paragraph{Headroom.} A numerical search for the best feasible policy on \res{ref.sites} random test sites found policies \res{ref.headroom}~\eur{} above the fixed reference on average; two repeated searches differed by \res{ref.err.lo} and \res{ref.err.hi}~\eur{}, so the best-known values are lower bounds with that error. On these sites the baseline's final policies were \res{ref.baseline.vsfixed} relative to the fixed reference and the models' \res{ref.glm.vsfixed} (GLM), \res{ref.mimo.vsfixed} (MiMo) and \res{ref.deepseek.vsfixed} (DeepSeek): most of the available improvement was left unused by every method.

\paragraph{Sensitivity.} The E1 comparison depends on two environment assumptions. With the side-wall heat exchange set to 4~W/m$^2$K instead of each site's drawn value (1--3) on \res{ueff.sites} random sites, the model-minus-baseline differences turned positive (DeepSeek \res{ueff.4.deepseek}, GLM \res{ueff.4.glm}, MiMo \res{ueff.4.mimo}~\eur{}, against \res{ueff.main.deepseek}, \res{ueff.main.glm} and \res{ueff.main.mimo} on the same sites at the drawn value; at 0~W/m$^2$K \res{ueff.0.deepseek}, \res{ueff.0.glm}, \res{ueff.0.mimo}); the method ranking changed in both settings. The models' policies use less light and more heat than the baseline's, and a larger exchange with the warmer neighbouring spaces favours them. With a fruit dry-matter fraction of \setting{0.07} instead of \setting{0.06} the E1 differences shrank to \res{dm.0.07.deepseek} (DeepSeek), \res{dm.0.07.glm} (GLM) and \res{dm.0.07.mimo} (MiMo); at \setting{0.05} they grew to \res{dm.0.05.deepseek}, \res{dm.0.05.glm} and \res{dm.0.05.mimo}. E1 is therefore a statement about the specified facility and price scenario, not a general ranking; E2 was insensitive to the dry-matter fraction.
