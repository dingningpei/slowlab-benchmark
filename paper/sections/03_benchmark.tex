\section{The SlowLab benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
\subsection{Design principles}
\label{sec:principles}
SlowLab is designed around what makes experiments in physical facilities hard, and each property is a requirement on the environment. Experiments are \textbf{Slow}: a crop occupies a compartment for 180 days, so a 365-day campaign leaves room for about two waves. They are \textbf{Irreversible}: starting a crop commits a compartment, and stopping it discards the crop without salvage. They are \textbf{Facility-bound}: four compartments must be shared, in parallel and on schedules the agent chooses. Their evidence is \textbf{Delayed and partial}: a crop's margin is known only when it closes, and while it grows only noisy process measurements exist. The site is \textbf{Hidden}: cultivar, facility and prices are drawn per site and never shown, so the agent must learn the site from its own experiments. Scoring is \textbf{Private and out of sample}: the recommended policy is scored in weather the agent never saw. No benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:related} combines the first four; the last two keep the score from rewarding memorisation of the site or of the campaign weather.

\subsection{Task}
\label{sec:formal}
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} follows one campaign. The agent system under test, a language model in a harness or a numerical optimiser, runs a four-compartment greenhouse facility for one year in a loop: it plans (step 1); starts crops under climate policies of its choice, stops them or advances the clock (2); lets the crops grow in compartments it cannot see into (3); reads what its feedback condition allows (4); and analyses its own data with tools that cost no campaign time (5). After day 365 it recommends one policy, which a private evaluator scores (6).

Formally, a site $s$ draws hidden parameters $\theta_s$ from a fixed distribution (Section~\ref{sec:env}) and a campaign weather year. The facility has $K=4$ compartments and the campaign lasts $T=365$ days. A policy $\pi\in\Pi$ fixes six set-points for a whole crop: day and night temperature, CO$_2$ target, supplemental light hours, ventilation temperature offset and ventilation humidity threshold, inside a box with night temperature not above day temperature. An experiment $(k,t_0,\pi)$ grows a crop under $\pi$ in compartment $k$ from day $t_0\le 183$ for 180 days, followed by 2 days of cleaning; a stop at $t<t_0+180$ discards it. When a crop closes, its contribution margin is released: harvest revenue minus the cost of electricity, heat, CO$_2$, planting, cleaning and daily overhead, at the site's public prices. While it runs, eight channels (climate, a canopy proxy, cumulative harvest and resource use) are measured every 300~s. Under \emph{Full} feedback any record measured so far can be read at any time; under \emph{Endpoint} feedback the agent sees only each compartment's status and each crop's totals at closure. Because a language model's replies are sampled, each of its campaigns is \emph{branched}: the day-0 design is made once without knowing the condition, then a Full and an Endpoint branch continue from it, differing only in feedback. A campaign allows at most 16 crop starts, 32 decision calls (start, stop, recommend) and 128 interface calls; a language-model agent may also send at most $5\times10^6$ prompt characters.

\subsection{The greenhouse behind the executor}
\label{sec:env}
The compartments in step 3 are 96~m$^2$ greenhouses, the size of research compartments used in tomato trials, simulated with the GreenLight model \citep{katzin2020greenlight} under historical outdoor weather from the Cabauw observatory \citep{knmi_cabauw,knmi_cabauw_rad} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:task}). A site draws cultivar parameters (temperature window, photosynthetic capacity, fruit growth, earliness, maximum LAI), facility parameters (cover transmission, leakage, ventilation, side-wall heat exchange, LED efficacy, deep-soil temperature), prices and small per-compartment differences. The distribution was fixed from physical and economic plausibility before any method was run and is never filtered on outcomes. Each site uses one campaign year and three evaluation years drawn without replacement from eleven audited years (2002--2011, 2015); development work used separate years and a separate master seed.

\subsection{Agent interface}
\label{sec:interface}
In steps 2 and 4 the agent acts only through the executor: five actions (start, stop, advance, observe, recommend), with replies that contain only what the feedback condition allows. The analysis tools of step 5 summarise the agent's own data (a crop table, channel summaries, a Gaussian-process fit of margin on policy and planting date with predictions, a process predictor of a running crop's final margin trained on development sites, and a space-filling candidate generator); they do not advance time. For language models a harness turns step 1 into calls: each carries the task, the tool catalogue, a digest of the state, the six most recent turns in full and a 2{,}000-character notebook the model maintains, and the model answers with one JSON object (Appendix~\ref{app:interface}). Malformed replies and invalid actions count as the method's own result; when the budget is nearly spent, the harness advances to the final day and accepts only a recommendation, falling back to the pre-experiment recommendation. The shields in Figure~\ref{fig:framework} are an information firewall, which rejects any outgoing message that would reveal hidden site information, and an audit that stores every message with its hash, so every campaign can be replayed.

\subsection{Scoring and reference points}
\label{sec:refs}
In step 6 the recommended policy $\hat\pi$ receives the score
\[
J_s(\hat\pi)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W_s}\ \sum_{d\in\{\text{1 Jan},\,\text{2 Jul}\}} m\!\left(\hat\pi,d;\theta_s,w\right),
\]
where $m(\pi,d;\theta_s,w)$ is the contribution margin per m$^2$ of a crop grown under $\pi$ from planting date $d$ on site $s$ in weather year $w$, and $W_s$ holds three evaluation years not used in the campaign. The agent knows this rule from the task description, but the evaluator is private and the score is never shown to it; the benchmark score is the mean of $J_s$ over unseen sites. A score in \eur{} means little on its own, so two references that use no agent bracket it. The \emph{fixed reference} applies a standard grower setting, chosen on development sites, without experiments; beating it shows that a year of experiments paid off. The \emph{best-known reference} is the best policy found by a numerical search on a random subset of test sites; it shows how much room is left above the fixed reference and is a lower bound on the attainable score.

\subsection{Validity of the benchmark}
\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{The score separates strategies.} On the test sites the fixed reference scored \res{mean.fixed.reference}~\eur{}, the main baseline (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) \res{mean.baseline.full}, and the best-known policies lay \res{ref.headroom}~\eur{} above the fixed reference on the searched sites, so the scale has room both above and below a competent method. With three evaluation years, the per-site standard error of a paired difference between two policies is \res{valid.evalerr.paired}~\eur{} on development sites, well below the 2~\eur{} smallest effect of interest; with \res{sites} sites the smallest differences detectable with 80\% power ranged from \res{range.mde.lo} to \res{range.mde.hi}~\eur{} across the primary tests.
\paragraph{Runs are reproducible.} The simulator, the evaluator and the harness are deterministic given the recorded model replies. \res{valid.trace.campaigns} formal campaigns replayed on a different host produced \res{valid.trace.identical} traces and settlements, and \res{valid.replay.evals} formal evaluations rerun after the study were \res{valid.replay.identical}.
