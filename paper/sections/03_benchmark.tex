\section{The SlowLab benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
\subsection{Design principles}
\label{sec:principles}
SlowLab is designed around what makes experiments in physical facilities hard, and each property is a requirement on the environment. Experiments are \textbf{Slow}: a crop occupies a compartment for 180 days, so a 365-day campaign leaves room for about two waves. They are \textbf{Irreversible}: starting a crop commits a compartment, and stopping it discards the crop without salvage. They are \textbf{Facility-bound}: four compartments must be shared, in parallel and on schedules the agent chooses. Their evidence is \textbf{Delayed and partial}: a crop's margin is known only when it closes, and while it grows only noisy process measurements exist. The site is \textbf{Hidden}: cultivar, facility and prices are drawn per site and never shown, so the agent must learn the site from its own experiments. Scoring is \textbf{Private and out of sample}: the recommended policy is scored in weather the agent never saw. No benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:related} combines the first four; the last two keep the score from rewarding memorisation of the site or of the campaign weather.

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:formal}
A site $s$ draws hidden parameters $\theta_s$ (cultivar, facility and prices; Section~\ref{sec:steps}) and a campaign weather year. An agent system, a language model in a harness or a numerical optimiser, runs a facility of $K=4$ compartments for $T=365$ days. A policy $\pi\in\Pi$ fixes six set-points for a whole crop: day and night temperature, CO$_2$ target, supplemental light hours, ventilation temperature offset and ventilation humidity threshold, inside a box with night temperature not above day temperature. An experiment $(k,t_0,\pi)$ grows a crop under $\pi$ in compartment $k$ from day $t_0\le 183$ for 180 days, followed by 2 days of cleaning; a stop at $t<t_0+180$ discards it. Let $m(\pi,d;\theta_s,w)$ be the contribution margin per m$^2$ of a crop grown under $\pi$ from planting date $d$ on site $s$ in weather year $w$: harvest revenue minus the cost of electricity, heat, CO$_2$, planting, cleaning and daily overhead, at the site's public prices. An experiment's margin is released only when it closes. After day $T$ the agent recommends one policy $\hat\pi$, scored as
\[
J_s(\hat\pi)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{w\in W_s}\ \sum_{d\in\{\text{1 Jan},\,\text{2 Jul}\}} m\!\left(\hat\pi,d;\theta_s,w\right),
\]
where $W_s$ holds three evaluation weather years not used in the campaign. The agent knows this rule from the task description, but the evaluator is private and the score is never shown to it. The benchmark score is the mean of $J_s$ over unseen sites. A campaign allows at most 16 experiments, 32 decision calls (start, stop, recommend) and 128 interface calls; a language-model agent may also send at most $5\times10^6$ prompt characters.

\subsection{A campaign step by step}
\label{sec:steps}
\label{sec:env}
\label{sec:interface}
\paragraph{Plan and act (steps 1--2).} The agent acts only through the executor, with five actions: start an experiment $(k,t_0,\pi)$, stop a running crop, advance the clock, observe a channel and recommend $\hat\pi$. For language models a harness turns planning into calls: each carries the task, the tool catalogue, a digest of the state, the six most recent turns in full and a 2{,}000-character notebook the model maintains, and the model answers with one JSON object (Appendix~\ref{app:interface}). Malformed replies and invalid actions count as the method's own result; when the budget is nearly spent, the harness advances to day $T$ and accepts only a recommendation, falling back to the pre-experiment recommendation.

\paragraph{Grow (step 3).} The compartments are 96~m$^2$ greenhouses, the size of research compartments used in tomato trials, simulated with the GreenLight model \citep{katzin2020greenlight} under historical outdoor weather from the Cabauw observatory \citep{knmi_cabauw,knmi_cabauw_rad} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:task}). The site parameters $\theta_s$ hold cultivar parameters (temperature window, photosynthetic capacity, fruit growth, earliness, maximum LAI), facility parameters (cover transmission, leakage, ventilation, side-wall heat exchange, LED efficacy, deep-soil temperature), prices and small per-compartment differences. Their distribution was fixed from physical and economic plausibility before any method was run and is never filtered on outcomes. Each site uses one campaign year and the three years of $W_s$, drawn without replacement from eleven audited years (2002--2011, 2015); development work used separate years and a separate master seed.

\paragraph{Observe (step 4).} While a crop runs, eight channels (climate, a canopy proxy, cumulative harvest and resource use) are measured every 300~s. Under \emph{Full} feedback any record measured so far can be read at any time; under \emph{Endpoint} feedback the agent sees only each compartment's status and each crop's totals at closure. Because a language model's replies are sampled, each of its campaigns is \emph{branched}: the day-0 design is made once without knowing the condition, then a Full and an Endpoint branch continue from it, differing only in feedback. An information firewall rejects any outgoing message that would reveal hidden site information, and an audit stores every message with its hash, so every campaign can be replayed.

\paragraph{Analyse (step 5).} Analysis tools summarise the agent's own data: a crop table, channel summaries, a Gaussian-process fit of margin on $(\pi,t_0)$ with predictions, a process predictor of a running crop's final margin trained on development sites, and a space-filling candidate generator over $\Pi$. They do not advance time.

\paragraph{Recommend (step 6).} After day $T$ the agent submits $\hat\pi$, and the private evaluator computes $J_s(\hat\pi)$ by simulating the two scoring crops in each year of $W_s$.

\subsection{Reference points}
\label{sec:refs}
A score in \eur{} means little on its own, so two references that use no agent bracket it. The \emph{fixed reference} applies a standard grower setting, chosen on development sites, without experiments; beating it shows that a year of experiments paid off. The \emph{best-known reference} is the best policy found by a numerical search on a random subset of test sites; it shows how much room is left above the fixed reference and is a lower bound on the attainable score.

\subsection{Validity of the benchmark}
\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{The score separates strategies.} On the test sites the fixed reference scored \res{mean.fixed.reference}~\eur{}, the main baseline (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) \res{mean.baseline.full}, and the best-known policies lay \res{ref.headroom}~\eur{} above the fixed reference on the searched sites, so the scale has room both above and below a competent method. With three evaluation years, the per-site standard error of a paired difference between two policies is \res{valid.evalerr.paired}~\eur{} on development sites, well below the 2~\eur{} smallest effect of interest; with \res{sites} sites the smallest differences detectable with 80\% power ranged from \res{range.mde.lo} to \res{range.mde.hi}~\eur{} across the primary tests.
\paragraph{Runs are reproducible.} The simulator, the evaluator and the harness are deterministic given the recorded model replies. \res{valid.trace.campaigns} formal campaigns replayed on a different host produced \res{valid.trace.identical} traces and settlements, and \res{valid.replay.evals} formal evaluations rerun after the study were \res{valid.replay.identical}.
