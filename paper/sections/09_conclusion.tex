\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\slowlab{} evaluates an agent as the operator of a resource-limited experimental campaign:
it must allocate treatments to a tiered facility, interpret time-stamped evidence and issue
a final recommendation while poor experiments incur economic loss. Version~2 makes
within-cycle observation explicit and evaluates designs and recommendations against the
same visible history.

The experiments give a narrower and better-supported result than the Version~1 mechanism
story. Fixed-history replay shows that allocation and reading are empirically separable but
task dependent. In 928 frozen-protocol episodes, standard design and inference aids do not
reliably reduce final regret for the tested Luna and Sol conditions. A fresh 384-episode
noise extension reproduces that conclusion at twice the observation noise and finds a
harmful design-aid effect at half noise. Within-cycle access is used by the agent but does
not significantly improve the final recommendation under a synchronous action space.

These findings motivate benchmarks that expose the resource and timing choices through
which better reasoning can affect an experiment. The next decisive extensions are matched
scripted-and-model comparisons on the same sites, a calibrated higher-dimensional history
evaluator, and asynchronous actions whose use of interim evidence can change physical
capacity rather than only an interim recommendation.
