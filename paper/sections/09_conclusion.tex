\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\slowlab{} evaluates an agent as the operator of a resource-limited experimental campaign:
it must allocate treatments to a tiered facility, interpret time-stamped evidence and issue
a final recommendation while poor experiments incur economic loss. Version~2 makes
within-cycle observation explicit and evaluates designs and recommendations against the
same visible history.

The experiments support a more specific result than a universal tool null. Fixed-history
replay shows that allocation and reading are empirically separable but task dependent. In
the original 928-episode protocol, standard aids do not reliably reduce final regret for
the tested Luna and Sol conditions. A fresh 384-episode noise extension reproduces the Luna
null at twice the observation noise and finds a harmful design-aid effect at half noise. A
prospective 288-episode same-site extension then shows that inference-aid value is model
dependent: it is null for Luna, directionally favorable but uncertain for DeepSeek V4.1
Flash, and beneficial for Qwen 3.8 27B. Under inference, the pairwise final-regret model
contrasts are no longer significant. Within-cycle access is used by the agent but does not
significantly improve the final recommendation under the current synchronous action space.

These findings motivate benchmarks that expose the resource and timing choices through
which better reasoning can affect an experiment. The next decisive extensions are matched
scripted-and-model comparisons on the same sites, replication of the observed model--tool
interaction, a calibrated higher-dimensional history evaluator, and asynchronous actions
whose use of interim evidence can change physical capacity rather than only an interim
recommendation.
