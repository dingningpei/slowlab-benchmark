\begin{abstract}
AI agents are increasingly proposed as autonomous experimenters, yet the benchmarks that test them mostly offer cheap experiments that answer at once. Experiments in physical facilities are different: they take months, occupy scarce equipment and cannot be undone. We present SlowLab, a benchmark in which an agent runs a one-year campaign in four simulated greenhouse compartments on a site whose crop, facility and prices are hidden. The agent decides when to plant which climate policy, whether to stop a crop and which measurements to read while crops grow, and at the end recommends one policy, which a private evaluator scores in weather the agent never saw. In a preregistered study on \res{sites} unseen sites, three language-model agents ended near a standard grower setting and clearly below a Bayesian-optimisation baseline. They ran the facility as two synchronous batches, so measurements taken during the season never changed what they planted, and these measurements improved no method's result. Having each method re-read the evidence collected in a campaign did not change the recommendations significantly, which points to where the models start and which experiments they try. Code is released under the MIT licence.
\end{abstract}
