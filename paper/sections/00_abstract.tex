\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks for automated experimental design often make experiments immediate, free, and
repeatable. Real campaigns can instead combine delayed outcomes, irreducible noise,
resource costs, and commitments that cannot be rolled back. We introduce \slowlab, an
executable greenhouse benchmark in which agents allocate physically constrained units,
observe a crop while it develops, and recommend operating conditions under an economic
objective. Version~2 separates interim measurements from terminal outcomes: agents may
inspect currently measurable canopy, harvest, environment, and incurred-cost records, while
future yield and profit remain unavailable until they occur.

We also replace the Version~1 design and decision efficiency ratios with a common-history
risk decomposition. Given the exact time-stamped history visible to an agent, $b(H_t)$ is
the Bayes-optimal posterior risk and $g(H_t,\hat x_t)$ is the recommendation's nonnegative
posterior excess risk. A continuous crop--economics posterior is updated by the joint
observation model; independent posterior samples are used for action selection and held-out
evaluation. On the one-factor Sanity calibration, independent $32\times32$ product
posteriors agree to response-curve RMSE $0.00445$. This calibration does not yet establish
accuracy on the higher-dimensional tasks.

An audit of the original 800 episodes found a component-information bypass, collapse of the
finite-atom evaluator, and a density-normalisation error that changed observation noise.
Those faults invalidate the published mechanism and tool-effect interpretations and cannot
be repaired by endpoint rescoring because observations drive later designs. We therefore
retain the Version~1 runs as an auditable archive and defer empirical claims about agent
failures to corrected Version~2 closed-loop experiments.
\end{abstract}
