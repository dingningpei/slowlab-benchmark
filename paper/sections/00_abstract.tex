\begin{abstract}
Scientific-agent benchmarks rarely test whether an agent can allocate a fixed set of
physical units, use evidence that arrives during long-running trials, and commit to a
final operating recommendation whose experimental cost is paid in the objective itself.
We introduce \slowlab, an executable controlled-environment horticulture benchmark with
split-plot controls, capped parallel capacity, correlated noise, irreversible 210-day
crop cycles, time-appropriate interim observations, and separate design and
recommendation actions.  We evaluate final and cumulative regret and use a
common-history Bayes-risk decomposition to diagnose what an executed design supports
and how much excess risk remains in the agent's recommendation; independent posterior
samples avoid in-sample action selection, and the calibrated one-factor case has
response-curve RMSE $0.00445$.  Across 928 episodes under a frozen protocol, neither
design nor inference aids significantly changed final regret for Luna on 56 paired
sites, and an inference aid did not improve Sol on 24 further sites; a fresh 384-episode
extension found the same null result at twice the observation noise, while a prespecified
secondary analysis found that the design aid increased regret at half noise
($\Delta=0.0113$, 95\% CI $[0.0053,0.0184]$, adjusted $p=0.0096$).
\end{abstract}
