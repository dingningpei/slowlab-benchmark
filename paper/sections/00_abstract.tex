\begin{abstract}
Experiments in physical facilities are slow, occupy scarce units and cannot be undone: a planted crop holds a greenhouse compartment for half a year, and stopping it destroys it. Benchmarks for experimenting agents mostly let them query cheap experiments that answer at once. We present SlowLab, a benchmark in which an agent runs a 365-day campaign in four simulated greenhouse compartments (GreenLight model, historical Cabauw weather), deciding when to plant which climate policy, whether to stop crops and what to read while they grow, and finally which policy to recommend; the recommendation is scored by a private evaluator in weather years the agent never saw, on sites whose cultivar, facility and prices are hidden. We pair the environment with a preregistered evaluation protocol, a prior-informed local Bayesian-optimisation baseline and validity checks showing that the score separates strategies and that runs replay bit for bit. On \res{sites} unseen sites (\res{campaigns} campaigns, USD \res{spend.total} of API cost), three low-cost language models ended at or below a standard grower setting and clearly below the baseline (\res{E1.deepseek.mean}, \res{E1.mimo.mean} and \res{E1.glm.mean}~\eur{}, all significant after Holm correction). Reading process measurements during the season improved no method's final policy (all Full$-$Endpoint differences between \res{range.E2.lo} and \res{range.E2.hi}~\eur{}), and the models ran the facility as two synchronous batches. Re-reading the same final evidence in a single prompt did not change recommendations significantly, which points to how the agents start and explore rather than how they read. Code is released under the MIT licence.
\end{abstract}
