\begin{abstract}
Experiments in physical facilities are slow, occupy scarce units and cannot be undone: a planted crop holds a greenhouse compartment for half a year, and stopping it destroys it. We present SlowLab, a benchmark in which an agent runs a 365-day campaign in four simulated greenhouse compartments (GreenLight model, historical Cabauw weather), choosing when to plant which climate policy, what to read while crops grow, and finally which policy to recommend; the recommendation is scored privately in independent weather years. In a preregistered run on \res{sites} unseen sites (\res{campaigns} campaigns, \res{evaluations} offline evaluations, USD \res{spend.total} of API cost), three low-cost language models improved on their own pre-experiment recommendations (significantly only DeepSeek) but ended at or below the level of a standard grower setting, and clearly below a simple prior-informed local Bayesian-optimisation baseline (differences \res{E1.deepseek.mean}, \res{E1.mimo.mean} and \res{E1.glm.mean}~\eur{}, all significant after Holm correction). Being able to read process measurements during the season did not improve the final policy for any method (all Full$-$Endpoint differences between \res{range.E2.lo} and \res{range.E2.hi}~\eur{}). Given the same final evidence in a single prompt, no Reader recommended significantly better or worse policies than the method that had collected it. On the sites where a numerical search estimated the available headroom (\res{ref.headroom}~\eur{} above the grower setting), the baseline's final policies gained \res{ref.baseline.vsfixed}~\eur{} and the models' none. The gap to the baseline narrows when the assumed boundary heat exchange is raised.
\end{abstract}
