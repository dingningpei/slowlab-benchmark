\begin{abstract}
Experiments in physical facilities are slow, occupy scarce units and cannot be undone: a planted crop holds a greenhouse compartment for half a year, and stopping it destroys it. We present SlowLab, a benchmark in which an agent runs a 365-day campaign in four simulated greenhouse compartments (GreenLight model, historical Cabauw weather), choosing when to plant which climate policy, what to read while crops grow, and finally which policy to recommend; the recommendation is scored privately in independent weather years. In a preregistered run on \res{sites} unseen sites (\res{campaigns} campaigns, \res{evaluations} offline evaluations, USD \res{spend.total} of API cost), three low-cost language models improved on their own pre-experiment recommendations but ended at about the level of a standard grower setting, and below a simple prior-informed local Bayesian-optimisation baseline (differences \res{E1.deepseek.mean}, \res{E1.mimo.mean} and \res{E1.glm.mean}~\eur{}; the first two significant after Holm correction). Being able to read process measurements during the season did not improve the final policy for any method (all Full$-$Endpoint differences between \res{E2.glm.mean} and \res{E2.baseline.mean}~\eur{}). Given the same final evidence in a single prompt, the models recommended worse policies than the campaigns that collected it, while every method left most of the headroom found by a numerical search unused. The E1 ranking is sensitive to the assumed boundary heat exchange and fruit dry-matter fraction.
\end{abstract}
