\section{Limitations}
SlowLab is a simulation study. The executor is not calibrated to a real greenhouse: an external check of GreenLight on observed actions from the 2019 Autonomous Greenhouse Challenge failed for temperature and humidity (Appendix~\ref{app:external}), and the sensor-noise model is a declared assumption. Results concern the tested facility, budget, action space and models; we make no claim about real greenhouses. The E1 gap narrows when the side-wall heat exchange is raised to 4~W/m$^2$K (where MiMo moves slightly ahead of the baseline) and with a higher fruit dry-matter fraction (Section~\ref{sec:e3}); only these two sensitivities were run, so no robustness claim is made for other assumptions (boundary temperatures, heating and ventilation targets, sensor bias, control step). Three low-cost models were tested; stronger or reasoning-heavy models may behave differently, and GLM ran with mandatory capped reasoning. The comparison is not knowledge-symmetric: the baseline starts from a grower setting chosen on development sites and uses a kernel fitted on development data, whereas the models start from their own prior knowledge; E1 therefore compares a domain-informed optimiser with general-purpose agents, not two blank-slate learners. The baseline was redesigned after the pilot because the plain BO scored below the fixed reference; it was tuned only on development sites, but the redesign is a pilot-informed decision. Its radius was selected at the largest of the three preregistered candidates, so a wider search might make it stronger still. Power came from eight pilot sites; we report the minimum detectable difference of every test. Campaigns ran in site order rather than random order. The run was completed in two parts (Appendix~\ref{app:original}): the corrected baseline was rerun and its radius re-selected after the first part's results existed, by the preregistered rule on development sites only; the \res{repair.branches} corrected language-model branches were continued from the point of the error days after they started, with the same model identifiers and providers, so a provider-side change in between cannot be excluded. BCa interval bounds for samples with many ties can differ between computing platforms; all reported analyses, original and corrected, were computed on one host (Appendix~\ref{app:original}).
