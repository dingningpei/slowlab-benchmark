\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the agents did and did not do.} The models designed reasonable first crops and their final policies were better than their pre-experiment recommendations, significantly so only for DeepSeek (Figure~\ref{fig:e1}, middle), but they did not use the facility as an instrument while it ran: a running crop was stopped only once, the Full and Endpoint branches usually planted the same second wave, and process readings and the process predictor were consulted a handful of times per campaign. The E2 null therefore reflects how the agents used the interface rather than a lack of information in it; the baseline, which by construction reads the process predictor at mid-season, did not gain from it either, which suggests that the in-season signal available through the predictor added little in this task.

\paragraph{Gathering versus reading evidence.} E3 was designed to separate the two, and it did not find a difference: a one-shot reading of the same final evidence recommended about as well as the campaign conversation that gathered it, and so did the baseline's numerical Reader. With 16 packets per source the test can only rule out large effects; within the models tested, neither step alone explains the gap to the baseline.

\paragraph{What the baseline result means.} A simple trust-region search that starts from a standard grower setting and recommends only what it tried beat the models in the specified facility and reached most of the headroom found by an offline numerical search, while the models stayed near the grower setting. Part of the gap is the starting point: the baseline starts from the fixed reference (\res{mean.fixed.reference}~\eur{}), which the models are not given, while their own pre-experiment recommendations scored \res{range.initial.lo}--\res{range.initial.hi}. Part is the search itself: within the year the baseline gained \res{vsfixed.baseline.mean}~\eur{} over its starting point, the models \res{range.learn.lo} to \res{range.learn.hi} over theirs. The baseline brings domain knowledge the models lack (a frozen kernel fitted on development data and the grower setting as anchor), so the result shows how far general-purpose agents are from a domain-informed optimiser in this task, not that language models cannot do better. Its policies also differ in operating style (more light, less heat), and the gap narrows when neighbouring spaces exchange more heat. SlowLab is therefore informative about how agents run asynchronous, irreversible experiments, and its E1 numbers should be read together with the sensitivity analysis rather than as a general ranking of methods for greenhouse control.
