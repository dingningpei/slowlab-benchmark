\section{Tasks and evaluation}
\label{sec:tasks-eval}

\subsection{Controlled-environment horticulture}
\label{sec:domain}

The current benchmark contains one validated domain. Its response model is the reduced
state-variable TOMGRO model \citep{jones1999reduced}; a site seed draws crop and economic
parameters and fixes the corresponding response surface. Climate chambers are blocks and
independently plumbed hydroponic loops are units. Temperature, photoperiod, lamp intensity
and carbon-dioxide enrichment are chamber-level controls; planting density and the pruning
target are loop-level controls. The objective is net profit per square metre per day. This
construction makes the simulator traceable to a published crop model, while Appendix
\ref{app:domain} records the additional choices made for parameter distributions, factor
ranges, prices, observation noise and facility size.

\subsection{Tasks}
\label{sec:tasks}

Four tasks vary the decision geometry while retaining the same crop-cycle duration,
commitment rule and economic objective. \textsc{Sanity} is a one-factor interface and
evaluator check. \textsc{Screen} allocates a sparse two-round budget across all six factors.
\textsc{Optimise} varies one chamber-level and one loop-level factor, so a feasible batch is
a split-plot allocation rather than an unordered list of points. \textsc{Transfer} optimizes
four resource factors and then asks for a new recommendation after electricity and gas
prices rise by $2.2\times$, without allowing another experiment. Table~\ref{tab:tasks-v2}
states the resource structure directly; we do not reuse the Version~1 task-difficulty ratios.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Following the information boundary of \S\ref{sec:roles}, evaluation has two layers. The
\emph{outcome evaluator} uses the hidden site to score what the campaign did: recommendation
regret and the opportunity cost of its executed experiments. These exact simulator outcomes
support the primary comparisons. The \emph{common-history evaluator} is a calibrated
benchmark-side diagnostic: it conditions on the same $H_t$ visible to the reader, while
using the known instance distribution and forward model to ask what that history could have
supported. It never supplies a posterior, action or score to the agent. The layers are
reported separately and never combined.

\subsubsection{Realised regret}
\label{sec:outcome}

Both quantities of \S\ref{sec:conditions} are made precise here. They are regret against
the same optimum in the same currency, because the experiment is paid for in the units of
what it is trying to maximise. The first is \eqref{eq:problem}, elicited after
\emph{every} round rather than only at $R$, and averaged over instances:
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathcal{R}}(r) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim P_\Theta}
\Big[\, f_\theta(x^\star_\theta) - f_\theta(\hat{x}_r) \,\Big],
\qquad x^\star_\theta \in \arg\max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} f_\theta(x).
\label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
The second is what the experiments themselves forwent: every occupied slot is run at the
treatment it was given, so a slot assigned a poor one costs the gap to the optimum for a
full cycle of length $\Delta$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{cum}} \;=\; \Delta \sum_{r=1}^{R} \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}_r}
\Big[\, f_\theta(x^\star_\theta) - f_\theta(x_u) \,\Big].
\label{eq:cumregret}
\end{equation}
This is the usual simple-versus-cumulative pair, and the second term is usually dropped in
benchmark settings because a trial there costs nothing. Here an agent that locates the optimum
by first running every bad treatment has answered the question and exhausted the facility
that paid for it. Neither
implies the other, and in our runs they do order agents differently
(Appendix~\ref{app:refs}); an agent that runs no experiments forgoes nothing and is judged
by \eqref{eq:regret} alone.

\subsubsection{Risk conditioned on the visible history}
\label{sec:eig}

The common-history evaluator compares design support and recommendation quality against the
same information set. Let
$H_t$ contain every submitted design, completed result, within-cycle measurement and
recommendation visible by simulation time $t$, and define
$R(\theta,x)=f_\theta(x^\star_\theta)-f_\theta(x)$.  The Bayes risk remaining after that
history is
\begin{equation}
b(H_t)=\min_{x\in\mathcal X}\mathbb E[R(\theta,x)\mid H_t].
\label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
For the agent's contemporaneous recommendation $\hat x_t$, its posterior risk and excess
risk are
\begin{equation}
a(H_t,\hat x_t)=\mathbb E[R(\theta,\hat x_t)\mid H_t],\qquad
g(H_t,\hat x_t)=a(H_t,\hat x_t)-b(H_t)\geq0.
\label{eq:capture}
\end{equation}
The numerical action set always includes $\hat x_t$, so the inequality follows from the
minimisation rather than from clipping.  The conditional value of a newly committed design
$D$ is $v(D\mid H)=b(H)-\mathbb E[b(H,D,Y_D)\mid H,D]$; it conditions on earlier evidence
instead of restarting from the prior each round.

The quantities in \eqref{eq:capture} replace the Version~1 ratios called design efficiency
$c$ and decision efficiency $\eta$, which are
withdrawn.  Their numerators were prior, preposterior design scores while their denominators
used realised recommendations, so $\eta$ was not a fraction of acquired information.  A
correlation between $c$ and $\eta$, movement in opposite directions, or their product does
not identify independent design and reader failures.  Likewise, identifying $\theta$ while
sampling far from the optimum need not imply low decision value: the value depends on the
posterior action, not the physical distance between sampled treatments and the optimum.

\paragraph{Estimation.}\label{sec:estimator} Crop physiology and site economics are updated
as independent continuous priors from canopy/harvest and cost records respectively;
terminal revenue then couples their product posterior.  One posterior sample selects the
Bayes action and an independent sample evaluates it and the agent recommendation.  Plant
variation is integrated with common stratified draws, and chamber, loop, batch, measurement
and reporting-resolution covariance retain their actual scopes.  Finite candidate search
provides only a feasible lower bound on each world's optimum and is labelled as such.  We
report seeds, effective sample sizes, predictive checks and between-run error, and suppress
quantitative decompositions whose calibration error is not below the effect being discussed.
