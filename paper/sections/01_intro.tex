\section{Introduction}
Language-model agents are increasingly proposed as autonomous experimenters. Most evaluations let the agent query an oracle cheaply and repeatedly. Real facility experiments differ in three ways that change the decision problem: an experiment occupies a unit for a long time, it cannot be revised once started (a crop can only be continued or destroyed), and its outcome becomes fully known only at the end, although partial process measurements are available while it runs.

SlowLab turns this setting into a reproducible benchmark. An agent manages four greenhouse compartments for one simulated year. It decides when to start crops under which of a six-dimensional family of climate policies, whether to stop them, and what to read from eight sensor channels; at the end it recommends one policy, which a private evaluator scores in three new weather years. Every site draws hidden cultivar, facility and price parameters, so the agent must learn the site from its own experiments.

We ask three questions, each answered by one preregistered experiment: \textbf{E1} how good is the final policy an agent finds, relative to a numerical baseline with the same interface and budget; \textbf{E2} does being able to read process measurements during the season (Full) improve on seeing only end-of-crop totals (Endpoint); \textbf{E3} are poor recommendations caused by poor evidence or by poor reading of the evidence. This draft reports E1 and E2. \todo{E3 after Phase 6.}

Contributions: (i) a facility-bound, asynchronous experimentation benchmark with a hidden-instance site distribution, a blinded agent interface and a private evaluator; (ii) a preregistered comparison of three language models with a prior-informed local BO baseline on \res{sites} new sites, with every identity, payload and cost recorded; (iii) the finding that the models learn within the year but do not reach the baseline, and that in-season process feedback is not used to advantage by any method.
